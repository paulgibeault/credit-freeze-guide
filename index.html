Credit Freeze Guide

Free by federal law. No accounts needed. Your data stays on your device.

Good to know: By federal law (the SECURE Act), credit freezes are 100% free. You should never have to pay to freeze or unfreeze your credit.

A credit freeze locks your credit file so no one can open new accounts in your name. You need to freeze your credit at all three bureaus. Each one gives you a PIN — write it down or save it in the "My PINs" tab.

What to have ready before you start

Each bureau will ask you to create an account and verify your identity. Have this information ready:

  • Full legal name (as it appears on your official documents)
  • Social Security number
  • Current mailing address
  • Previous address (if you moved within the last 2 years)
  • Some credit history details — a few bureaus ask about existing accounts or streets you've lived on

Having this ready makes the process take just a few minutes per bureau.

What happens after you freeze?

  • New accounts: No one can open new credit in your name while the freeze is active.
  • Existing accounts: Your current credit cards, loans, and accounts are unaffected.
  • Temporarily unlock: If you need to apply for credit, you can temporarily lift the freeze.
  • Cost: Always free — by federal law.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about credit freezes, based on information from each bureau.

How long does it take to place a freeze?

Online: Usually just a few minutes — the freeze takes effect immediately. By phone: Up to 1 hour. By mail: Up to 3 business days after they receive your request.

Does a credit freeze affect my credit score?

No. A credit freeze has zero impact on your credit score. You can freeze and unfreeze as often as you like.

Can I still use my existing credit cards and loans?

Yes. A freeze only stops new accounts from being opened. Your existing credit cards, mortgages, auto loans, and other accounts continue to work normally. The companies can still take payments and report to bureaus.

How do I temporarily lift a freeze?

Sign in to the bureau's website (use the buttons above), look for "Lift a freeze" or "Temporary freeze removal," and enter your PIN. You can choose to lift it for a specific time period (like 1 hour or 1 day) or for a specific company. Most lifts take effect instantly online.

Is a credit freeze the same as a fraud alert?

No. A fraud alert lasts 1 year (7 years for verified identity theft) and requires companies to verify your identity before granting credit. A credit freeze stays in place until you remove it and is stronger — it blocks access to your credit file entirely. A freeze is the better protection.

How much does it cost to freeze or unfreeze my credit?

Nothing. By federal law (the SECURE Act), freezing and unfreezing your credit is always free. If a bureau asks you to pay, something is wrong — ask to speak to a supervisor or contact the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau.

What if I lose my PIN?

You can retrieve it by logging into your account on the bureau's website. Each bureau lets you view or reset your PIN after you verify your identity. That's why it's helpful to save it in this app or write it down.

Do I need to freeze all three bureaus?

Yes. Each bureau maintains its own separate credit file, and not all lenders report to all three bureaus. To be fully protected, you need to freeze at all three: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

Can a freeze stop someone from getting a job or apartment?

No. A freeze only affects new credit applications. Background checks for employment, renting an apartment (in most cases), and other non-credit inquiries are not blocked. If someone does need to check your credit, you can temporarily lift the freeze for them.

How do I know the freeze actually went through?

Each bureau sends a confirmation (usually by email or on-screen) with your PIN and a confirmation number after you freeze. Save that confirmation. You can also call the bureau to verify the freeze is active.

How to unfreeze temporarily

If you need to apply for credit (a loan, apartment, etc.), you can temporarily lift your freeze:

  1. Sign in to each bureau's website using the buttons above
  2. Look for "Lift a freeze" or "Temporary freeze removal"
  3. You'll need your PIN to do this
  4. Choose how long you need the freeze lifted (you can set a specific date range)

Some people offer a "permanent" freeze removal option. This is permanent — to freeze again, you'd need to contact each bureau. Unless you never want another freeze, choose the temporary option instead.
